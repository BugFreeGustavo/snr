This document describes how independent professionals organise and execute work in real-world environments.

The objective is not to define how Professionals should work, but to understand how they actually work.

This understanding forms the foundation of the SNR domain model.

## Why Workflows Matter

Professional work is rarely linear.

Projects evolve.
Requirements change.
Dependencies appear unexpectedly.
Clients adjust priorities.
Deliverables arrive incrementally.

Despite these variations, recurring patterns emerge across almost every professional engagement.

Understanding these patterns allows SNR to model the business instead of imposing rigid software processes.

The workflows described in this document represent observed reality rather than mandatory procedures.

---

## Workflow Principles

Every workflow documented here follows the same principles.

- Professionals own their workflow.
- Clients define expected outcomes rather than internal processes.
- Deliverables represent business commitments.
- Dependencies introduce uncertainty.
- Communication continuously influences priorities.
- Knowledge accumulated during one engagement increases efficiency in future engagements.

SNR supports these realities without attempting to replace them.

# Business Journey 01

## Recurring Production Project

### Context

Recurring productions represent long-term professional relationships between a Professional and a Client.

Although every production cycle is technically a new Project, the business relationship, expectations and operational patterns remain largely consistent over time.

Television productions are a common example.

Each season is delivered independently while preserving the knowledge accumulated from previous seasons.

---

### Goal

Deliver recurring work within predictable deadlines while preserving operational knowledge that improves future production cycles.

---

### Example

An audio post-production Professional is hired to work on **Got Talent 2026**.

Before work begins, the Client and Professional agree on the commercial and operational expectations.

Typical questions include:

- How many episodes will be produced?
- What is the average duration of each episode?
- What is the delivery schedule?
- What is the agreed price per episode?
- When will production materials become available?

At this stage, both parties understand the commercial agreement before operational work begins.

---

### Typical Timeline

A typical production week may evolve as follows.

**Monday**

The video post-production supervisor communicates the production schedule for the week.

**Tuesday**

The first batch of materials becomes available.

Approximately half of the episode is delivered.

**Wednesday**

Additional edited footage becomes available.

The Professional continues working while integrating the newly received material.

**Thursday**

The remaining content and promotional teaser are delivered.

The Professional completes the full episode.

**Friday Morning**

The completed audio post-production is delivered internally for review by the production team and director.

**Friday Afternoon**

Final comments, editorial changes and Client feedback are received.

Approved changes are incorporated before final delivery.

The production episode is then considered complete.

The following week, the entire cycle begins again.

---

### Business Characteristics

This type of work demonstrates several recurring business characteristics.

- Deliverables arrive incrementally.
- Work starts before all materials are available.
- Dependencies continuously evolve.
- Client feedback generates additional work.
- Deadlines are predictable but non-negotiable.
- Knowledge accumulates every production cycle.

---

### Business Knowledge Generated

Every completed production generates reusable knowledge.

Examples include:

- Typical episode duration.
- Average turnaround time.
- Preferred delivery format.
- Client communication patterns.
- Internal review schedule.
- Pricing history.
- Seasonal production rhythm.
- Frequent technical requirements.

This knowledge reduces uncertainty during future seasons.

---

### Why This Matters

From a business perspective, every season is a new Project.

From an operational perspective, every season extends an existing professional relationship.

SNR preserves both perspectives simultaneously.

## Cross-Workflow Observations

Although every Professional develops a personal way of working, several patterns consistently emerge across professional engagements.

- Every workflow begins with a commercial agreement.
- Deliverables mark visible progress within a workflow.
- Dependencies force workflows to adapt rather than proceed linearly.
- Client feedback rarely represents the end of work.
- Knowledge generated during one Project increases confidence in future projects.
- Long-term Client relationships are built through repeated successful deliveries rather than individual projects.

These observations represent business truths rather than software requirements.

---

## Knowledge Preservation Principle

Every completed Project should leave behind reusable knowledge.

The knowledge generated during work is itself a valuable business asset.

SNR exists not only to organise ongoing work, but also to preserve experience for future engagements.
