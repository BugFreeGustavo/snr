## Purpose

This manifesto defines the philosophy behind SNR.

It is not a product specification, a roadmap or a technical document.

Instead, it captures the beliefs that shape every decision made throughout the lifetime of the product.

When priorities change, technologies evolve or new features are proposed, this document remains the philosophical reference for the project.

## We Believe

We believe independent professionals should spend their time creating value, not reconstructing information.

Freelance work is filled with administrative friction that slowly accumulates across projects.

Emails become archives.

Messages become forgotten decisions.

Folders become difficult to navigate.

Spreadsheets become historical records that are never consulted again.

The work is completed.

The experience disappears.

We believe this should not be the normal way of working.

---

## Experience Is More Valuable Than Information

Information is easy to store.

Experience is difficult to preserve.

Completing a project teaches lessons that rarely exist in a single document.

Clients develop preferences.

Processes evolve.

Problems are solved.

Estimates become more accurate.

These lessons represent operational knowledge.

We believe software should preserve that knowledge instead of allowing it to disappear.

---

## Every Project Should Improve the Next One

Completing a project should never reset the learning process.

Every project should enrich the next.

Knowledge should accumulate over time.

Experience should compound.

The value of the system should increase with every completed engagement.

---

## Simplicity Is a Product Decision

Complexity often enters software one feature at a time.

Each feature appears reasonable in isolation.

Together, they create noise.

We believe simplicity is achieved through deliberate restraint.

SNR should solve fewer problems exceptionally well instead of attempting to solve every problem adequately.

---

## Software Should Adapt to People

Freelancers do not share a single workflow.

Different professions.

Different clients.

Different methodologies.

Different habits.

SNR should support operational flexibility without imposing unnecessary processes.

The software should adapt to the user.

The user should not adapt to the software.

---

## Context Creates Understanding

Information without context quickly loses its value.

A client name means little without the project.

A credential means little without the service it unlocks.

A deliverable means little without the milestone it supports.

Relationships create meaning.

We believe preserving context is as important as preserving information itself.

---

## Documentation Is Part of the Product

Documentation is not an afterthought.

It is not something written after implementation.

Understanding precedes design.

Design precedes architecture.

Architecture precedes implementation.

Documentation is the first expression of the product.

Code is simply one implementation of that understanding.

---

## Focus Is a Competitive Advantage

SNR does not aspire to replace every productivity tool.

It does not seek to become another project management platform, CRM or accounting system.

Its value comes from doing one thing exceptionally well:

Preserving operational experience.

Every feature that weakens this focus weakens the product itself.

---

## Build Knowledge, Not Features

Features create functionality.

Knowledge creates leverage.

The objective of SNR is not to accumulate capabilities.

It is to accumulate operational knowledge that continuously improves future work.

That distinction defines the product.

---

## Closing Statement

SNR exists because experience deserves to be preserved.

Every completed project contains knowledge that should remain available long after the deliverables have been sent.

Our responsibility is not simply to build software.

Our responsibility is to build a product that helps independent professionals carry their experience forward.

Less noise.

More signal.

---

> **Experience should be preserved instead of recreated.**
